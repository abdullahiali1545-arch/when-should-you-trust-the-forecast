Six-hour air pollution forecasts for London, logged before the answer exists

A frozen forecasting model predicts fine-particle pollution (PM2.5) six hours ahead at four monitoring sites. Each forecast is written to a public, append-only log before the real reading is published, then scored when it arrives.

How it works

The model is frozen. A gradient-boosted model (LightGBM) trained once on 2018–2025 data and never retrained. Rebuilt from its recipe, it reproduces the evaluated model's forecasts exactly, and gives identical numbers on Windows and Linux.

The warning is simple on purpose. A site turns amber when the model's average error over the previous day is above a threshold fixed in advance. In backtesting, a more complex "watcher" model did no better than this rule.

The data is late. DEFRA publishes readings in a daily batch, roughly 8 to 15 hours behind. So forecasts are made from the newest published hour, for times that have passed but whose readings are not yet public.

Nothing is edited afterwards. Forecasts and scores go to separate append-only files on a branch that blocks rewrites. Every rule used here was committed to the project spec before the first live forecast.
